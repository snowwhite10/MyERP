Operational feasibility depends on whether users can follow the proposed processes and understand their outcomes. The project scope covers multi-company access, role and data-scope restrictions, document approvals, inventory movements, general-ledger posting, audit history, three interface languages and light and dark themes. These controls provide a design basis for separating duties and tracing a transaction from entry to result.

The planned operating flow is to establish authorized users and master data, enter documents, review or approve them where required, and post through the owning module. Lists, validation messages and batch results must explain empty states, rejected actions and partial outcomes. Training material should use the same terms as the localized interface and cover correction, cancellation, reconciliation and escalation. The business owner must validate the actual approval matrix, role assignments, cutover sequence and exception handling before adoption.

Implementation evidence is incremental: the progress record identifies delivered modules and outstanding work, while automated tests and QA records cover only the cases they have executed. The current SQL Server service is stopped in the development environment, so live database and default startup acceptance cannot be inferred from offline document generation. A pilot with representative users, real workflow scenarios and measured task completion is still required before declaring operational acceptance.

Risks include inaccurate master data, unclear ownership of cross-module records, resistance to changed approval steps and insufficient support for users. Mitigations are data ownership rules, role-based training, staged pilot use, visible audit and error feedback, and a documented support route. Production operation and migration of customer data remain outside the current committed scope.
